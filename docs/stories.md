## Case 01 — The Broken Hand

### Premise
A corporate data breach occurred at **2:47am** in a restricted server wing of an AuditCorp facility. The security guard assigned to that wing — **VALE-7**, known to colleagues as *Callum* — filed an incident report stating he was at his post the entire night. Surveillance footage covering a 40-minute window is corrupted.

The corporation suspects Callum allowed someone into the server wing. Your job is to reconstruct his whereabouts during those 40 minutes and render a verdict.

---

### Characters

**Callum (VALE-7)**
The subject. A security guard — quiet, professional, unremarkable on paper. He has no prior incidents on record. His incident report is clean, formal, and almost certainly a lie. He was not at his post. What he was doing instead is the heart of the case.

**Mara**
An undocumented cleaning worker employed under a false ID. She collapsed on shift during the 40-minute window. Callum found her, stayed with her in the medical bay hallway, and said nothing — because reporting her presence would have had her deported or detained. She never appears on camera. She exists in the record only as "unknown individual, escorted by facility guard" in a medical bay intake log.

**Director Voss**
The player's superior. He communicates via terminal memo and voice log. Cold, procedural, and precise. He requires mandatory notation at every checkpoint — framed as quality control. He will be reading. He is reading. He is not reading for the reasons the player thinks.

---

### The Central Lie
Callum lied about his location to protect Mara. He was not at his post. He was sitting outside the medical bay, alone, in the middle of the night, waiting to make sure she was okay. He told no one because telling anyone would have destroyed her.

He was not involved in the breach.

---

### The Twist
Late in the audit, a biomechanical motion sensor log surfaces from the medical bay hallway. It captures the moment Callum caught Mara before she hit the floor. The sensor recorded his elbow joint rotating to **127 degrees** in the catch — a movement that requires articulation beyond the human anatomical range. The human arm cannot produce that angle in that configuration.

Callum never knew. He just caught her.

The biological impossibility does not make the act less real. The game does not tell the player what to think about that.

---

### The Second Twist
Cross-referencing the medical bay timestamp (2:52am) with the server wing access card scan (2:51am) produces an irresolvable contradiction. Callum was in the medical bay at 2:52am. His card was scanned at the server wing at 2:51am — on the other side of the facility, four minutes away at a run.

Both fragments are verified. Both are true. The only explanation is that there were **two Callums** — the same base Sim model, running in parallel. One stayed with Mara. One accessed the server wing.

Which one is the real Callum? The one who showed compassion, or the one who committed the breach?

The corporation already knows the answer. They built both of them.

### Emotional Core
A Sim who doesn't know he's a Sim, performing an act of pure compassion, undone by the geometry of his own kindness. The question the case leaves unanswered: does the impossibility of the movement make the compassion less real?
